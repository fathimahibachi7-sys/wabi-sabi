Book 05

Manju

M. T. Vasudevan Nair

05 Manju M. T. Vasudevan Nair

On distance, waiting, atmosphere and things left unsaid.

What stayed with me

Manju is a very different kind of book from the others I chose. It is quiet and atmospheric, and instead of depending on a dramatic plot, it made me pay attention to the emotions and small moments in the characters' lives.

What stayed with me was Vimala's life in Nainital and the sense of distance surrounding her. I remember the elderly man who visits the hostel and the small interactions around her. These moments may seem simple, but they add to the feeling of waiting and uncertainty that runs throughout the story.

Another thing that stood out to me was the way Vimala's family contacts her regarding money. Even though she is living away from them, her family responsibilities and expectations continue to reach her. I found this interesting because it shows how difficult it can be to completely separate ourselves from the people and responsibilities connected to our lives.

I also liked the atmosphere of the novel. The cold and misty setting of Nainital seemed to reflect Vimala's state of mind. The mist felt like more than just part of the scenery — it added to the uncertainty and the feeling that some things in life remain unclear.

What I found interesting about Manju is that it does not explain everything directly. There is a sense of waiting, memories, uncertainty and things left unsaid. It made me slow down as a reader and think about what the characters might be feeling instead of simply following the events.

For me, Manju was a story about distance, waiting and the emotions that exist beneath ordinary moments. It stayed with me because of its atmosphere and the quiet way in which it presents the complicated inner lives of its characters.

It is a book that asks you to slow down and notice what is left unsaid.
