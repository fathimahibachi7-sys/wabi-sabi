The Diary of a Young Girl

Anne Frank

03 The Diary
of a Young Girl Anne Frank

On family, friendship, hope and finding a voice.

What stayed with me

Some books tell us a story, while some make us feel like we are quietly living alongside the person who wrote them. The Diary of a Young Girl felt like the second kind to me.

What stayed with me most was the contrast between the terrible circumstances Anne was living through and the fact that, at heart, she was still a teenage girl. She was living through fear, uncertainty and the hardships caused by Nazi persecution, yet she still had ordinary teenage thoughts, feelings, dreams, friendships and little things that mattered to her.

I was especially touched by the way Anne used her diary as a way of helping herself. She wrote about her thoughts, emotions, fears, hopes and relationships. I found it meaningful that she gave her diary the name Kitty and treated it almost like a trusted friend.

Her love for her family also stood out to me. Even while living through such difficult circumstances, her relationships remained important to her. Her friendships and her experiences as a young girl made the diary feel personal and relatable despite the extraordinary situation surrounding her.

What makes the book particularly powerful to me is this combination of the ordinary and the heartbreaking. Anne could write about everyday teenage feelings while living through one of the darkest periods in history.

After reading it, I felt that her diary was more than a record of what happened. It was a place where she could remain herself — a space where she could speak honestly, dream, complain, hope and simply be a teenager.

What stayed with me most was that even when so much was taken away from her, Anne still held on to her thoughts, relationships and voice.
